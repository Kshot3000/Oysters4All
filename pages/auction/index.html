List the auction

The auctioneer commits to the item terms, the minimum bid, the commit deadline and the reveal deadline (both block heights) in a tamper-evident descriptor. Publish the descriptor before the commit deadline — that is what makes the sealed-bid phase provably fair.

The auction rules (published with the descriptor)

  • One bidder, one commitment. A second commitment from the same address is flagged and both are excluded.
  • Commitment hashes must be published before the commit deadline block — late hashes are refused.
  • Bids are revealed after the commit deadline and before the reveal deadline. A reveal whose (bid, salt) does not reproduce the recorded commitment is refused loudly.
  • Revealed bids below the minimum are refused. Highest verified bid wins; ties are broken by earliest reveal.
  • The winner pays the seller directly, off-page. This page never moves PRL.

Commit a sealed bid

A bidder computes their commitment locally — address + bid + a random 16-byte salt, hashed as SHA-256("pearl-auction-commit:v1:⟨descriptorHash⟩:⟨address⟩:⟨bidGrains⟩:⟨saltHex⟩"). Then they publish only the hash. The bid stays secret until the reveal phase.

Reveal the bids

After the commit deadline and before the reveal deadline, each bidder reveals their bid + salt. The page re-derives the commitment — a mismatch is refused loudly and the bid is rejected. Bids below the minimum are refused. Reveals are processed in order; ties go to the earliest reveal.

Honest limits

  • No PRL ever moves through this page. It lists auctions, generates and checks sealed-bid commitments, settles to a winner, and plans the payment — the winner pays the seller directly from their own wallet, off-page.
  • Fairness is a commitment protocol, not code: Pearl has no smart contracts, so nothing on-chain enforces the auction. The guarantee is that the descriptor must be published before the commit deadline — anyone can then re-check every commitment hash and redo every SHA-256 reveal by hand. A descriptor that appears only after the commit deadline proves nothing.
  • Bids are only as sealed as the bidder's discipline. Publishing the bid or the salt before the reveal deadline lets anyone read (or front-run) the bid. Commitments are hiding only while the salt stays secret.
  • The organizer's commitment ledger is manual: bidders publish their hashes publicly (X, Discord, the item page) and the organizer records them. Verify the ledger against those public posts for auctions that decide real money.
  • One bidder, one commitment. A second commitment from the same address flags the bidder and excludes both — but nothing stops one person from bidding under two different addresses. Know your bidders.
  • Blockbook is a third-party read source; for real-money deadlines, confirm heights with your own pearld node — the manual-height paths exist for exactly this. Block countdowns assume the 194-second target block time.
