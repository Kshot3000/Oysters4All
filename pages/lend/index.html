Pearl Lend

Collateralized PRL loans — the counting-house desk

Draft the loan offer

A Pearl loan is a pawn-shop loan and this desk is honest about it: the borrower locks collateral C in a Taproot vault, the lender sends principal P to the borrower, and the borrower repays P + interest to reclaim the collateral. Both are PRL, so there is no price oracle and no liquidation math to get wrong — the vault must always be able to pay the lender back, which is why collateral must cover the full repayment plus your chosen ratio. Two script paths move the coins: a 2-of-2 repay leaf (both sign the repayment) and a lender-only default leaf behind a CSV timelock of term + grace blocks. Interest is grain-exact integer math, computed once at origination.

Repay the loan

    
The borrower proposes the repayment — lender gets exactly principal + interest, the
    borrower keeps the change — signs it, and hands the proposal to the lender. The lender's copy of this desk
    re-verifies the borrower's signature against the repayment digest before co-signing; a bad signature
    refuses assembly, never a half-signed broadcast. Exchange the proposal JSON by any channel you trust.

Honest limits

    
    
      
  • No oracle, by design. Principal and collateral are both PRL, so there is no price to
      feed and no liquidation cascade. If you want cross-asset loans, this desk is not the tool.
    • 
      
  • The lender's protection is overcollateralization, not legal recourse. Collateral must
      cover the full repayment — a borrower who repays reclaims the excess; a borrower who defaults forfeits it.
    • 
      
  • CSV maturity is measured from the lock transaction's confirmation height. The default
      claim is consensus-invalid before term + grace blocks pass; the desk refuses to build it early
      unless you override explicitly.
    • 
      
  • Manual coordination. There is no messaging, no order book, no watchtower. Proposals and
      descriptors move by whatever channel you and your counterparty trust. The desk verifies, it does not vouch.
    • 
      
  • Keys. Nothing is stored: pasted keys live in memory only and are wiped after signing.
      Prefer signing in your own wallet and pasting only signatures and x-only pubkeys.
