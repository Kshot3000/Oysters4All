Honest limits — read before weaving

  • MuSig2 is n-of-n: every member must take part in every signing ceremony. If one key is lost, the funds are locked. This is not a threshold scheme.
  • The ceremony is interactive: members exchange nonce bundles and partial signatures with the coordinator. Anyone who sees a member's secret nonce (not the bundle — the secret) alongside their partial can recover that member's private key. Keep secrets on-device; share only bundles and partials.
  • Simulation is demo-grade: the in-page simulator uses throwaway keys and is for learning the flow only — it proves the math, not a real ceremony.
  • This page never moves PRL: it builds keys, addresses, and signatures. It does not construct transactions, hold custody, or broadcast anything.

Gather the weavers

A mesh holds 2–7 members. Each member contributes an x-only (32-byte) public key — pasted from any wallet, or derived here from a fresh local BIP-86 mnemonic (m/86'/808276'/0'/0/i). Only members whose key was derived on this device can sign in the guided ceremony below; pasted-key members sign on their own machines and paste their bundles back.

The woven key

Every member key is sorted, hashed into L, and weighted by its own key coefficient — this is what makes MuSig2 secure against rogue-key attacks. The weighted sum is normalized to even-y (the aggregate internal key), then TapTweaked into the final key the address pays to. Funds sent here look exactly like a single-key Taproot payment.

The signing ceremony

    
Three rounds, coordinated here: ① every member commits a pair of public
    nonces (a bundle) · ② the coordinator aggregates them into one challenge
    ③ every member signs locally with their secret key and the coordinator aggregates the
    partials into one BIP-340 signature. Members with local key badges commit
    and sign on this device; everyone else does it on their own machine and pastes their bundles back.
