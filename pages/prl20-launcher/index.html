Why two transactions?

A PRL-20 operation lives inside a Taproot inscription envelope — a script that reveals the JSON on-chain. If that script's hash were visible beforehand, anyone could front-run your deploy or mint. The commit → reveal pattern fixes this:

  1. Commit. You send PRL to a Taproot address derived from your key tweaked with the inscription script. The tweak commits to the script without revealing it — observers see an ordinary Taproot payment.
  2. Reveal. You spend that output with a script-path witness containing the envelope and a control block proving it was committed to. The JSON becomes public only now, and the inscription is assigned to the first output of the reveal.

Fees

You pay for both transactions: the commit (a small keypath spend) and the reveal (larger — it carries the whole inscription in its witness). The launcher estimates both from the actual serialized sizes and shows a full cost breakdown before you sign anything. PRLS mints additionally pay a 1 PRL launch fee to the operator's fee recipient.

Safety notes

  • Keys never leave your browser and are never stored. Reload the page and the wallet is gone.
  • Broadcasting is a separate, explicit click per transaction — building signs locally but sends nothing.
  • The reveal must wait for the commit to confirm (or be in the mempool with the 0-conf override).
  • Always verify the JSON in the preview — what you inscribe is final.
