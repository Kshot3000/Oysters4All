Pearl Atlas

A cartographer's desk for BIP-380 output descriptors on Pearl (PRL)

@kshot9000

Chart a descriptor

Paste any output descriptor. The desk parses it like Bitcoin Core would — grammar, checksum, keys, and the full Taproot tree — and charts what it finds. Nothing leaves your browser; there are no network calls at all.

Seal & verify checksums

BIP-380 checksums are the wax seal on a descriptor: 8 characters that catch any transcription error. Add one to a bare descriptor, or verify a sealed one.

Derive addresses

Give a ranged descriptor and an index range; the desk derives each address exactly as BIP-386 specifies. Only tr() and addr() produce Pearl-spendable addresses — anything else yields its exact scriptPubKey with an honest note.

Charted templates

Sound, well-trodden descriptor shapes. Placeholders like xpubA must be replaced with your own keys before use — the desk refuses to derive from placeholders.

Prove the desk

Don't take the brass plate's word for it. This runs the desk's pinned test battery in your browser: official BIP-380 checksum vectors, a known-answer BIP-86 address, control-block recomputation, and multipath expansion.

Honest limits of this desk

  • Fully offline. No network calls, no servers, no tracking. Your descriptors never leave this page.
  • Only tr() and addr() are Pearl-spendable. Legacy types (pkh, wpkh, sh, wsh, multi…) resolve to their exact scriptPubKey, labeled as not Taproot — Pearl is a Taproot-only chain.
  • Private keys stay in memory and are wiped. If you paste WIF or xprv material, a warning appears and the Wipe button clears every input. The desk never stores or transmits keys.
  • Descriptors are not wallets. This desk charts and derives; it does not sign, broadcast, or hold funds.
  • Verify before trusting. Checksums catch typos, not malice. The Verify tab replays the pinned vectors — run it.
